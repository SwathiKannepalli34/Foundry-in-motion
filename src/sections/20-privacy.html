20 · What happens to my data?

Data privacy and abuse monitoring in Foundry

The first question every customer asks. The honest answer depends on which model you use: models sold by Azure are operated by Azure under Microsoft's data protection terms, while partner and community models are Non-Microsoft Products with the provider's own terms. Abuse monitoring follows the same split.

1P and 3P in the docs. People say "first-party (1P)" and "third-party (3P)" models. The documentation uses Foundry Models sold by Azure (includes all Azure OpenAI models plus selected models from other providers) and Foundry Models from partners and community (for example Anthropic's Claude). This guide uses 1P and 3P as shorthand for those two.

The 1P lane runs entirely on Azure under the Microsoft DPA. In the 3P lane, the provider is the operator and processor, and where your data goes depends on the hosting option you choose.

For models sold by Azure, your prompts (inputs), completions (outputs), embeddings and training data:

  • Are not available to other customers.
  • Are not available to OpenAI or other providers of models sold by Azure.
  • Are not used by those providers to improve their models or services.
  • Are not used to train any generative AI foundation models without your permission or instruction.
  • Customer data, prompts and completions are not used to improve Microsoft or third-party products without your explicit permission.
  • Your fine-tuned models are available exclusively to you.
  • Microsoft hosts these models in its own Azure environment. They do not interact with services operated by the model providers, such as ChatGPT or the OpenAI API.
  • The models are stateless: no prompts or completions are stored in the model.
  • The Microsoft Products and Services Data Protection Addendum (DPA) governs the processing.

At rest is always your geography. For Global and Data Zone deployments, only the location of processing changes. Stored data, such as uploaded files and the abuse monitoring data store, stays in the geography you designated, and Azure data processing and compliance commitments still apply.

What is stored, and how it is protected

  • Stored data lives in the Foundry resource in your Azure tenant, in the same geography as the resource.
  • Encrypted at rest with AES-256 by default, with an option for a customer-managed key (some preview features may not support it). Microsoft-managed keys always provide baseline encryption.
  • You can delete stored data at any time.
  • For direct Responses API calls, the service manages the storage; it does not support bring-your-own storage for that state. Content in Cosmos DB or Blob Storage is encrypted, and the customer-managed key is used when configured and applicable.
  • Fine-tuned models are encrypted at rest when not deployed, exclusive to you, and deletable. Safety evaluations of fine-tuned models log only the assessment (deployable or not).
  • Preview features may follow different privacy practices, including for abuse monitoring, under the supplemental preview terms.

Foundry Agent Service adds tools and stored state, so there are more places data can go. The key points from the agent data privacy page:

Your data

  • Prompts, completions and your data are not available to other customers, and not available to or used to improve models from OpenAI, Meta, Cohere or Mistral.
  • Stored agent data lives at rest in the resource in your tenant and geography; it can be encrypted with a customer-managed key (preview features may not support it) and deleted by you.
  • With standard agent setup you bring your own Storage, AI Search and Cosmos DB so conversation state and files stay under your control.

Tools can change the rules

  • Tools that call external services follow those services' terms. For example, Grounding with Bing Search has its own data collection and privacy terms and is a Microsoft-as-controller service, excluded from the Microsoft DPA.
  • Data you pass in MCP tool headers is available only for the current run and is not persisted.
  • Publishing to Microsoft 365 and Teams means those services process and store some data under their own terms and residency commitments.
Four things developers must handle.
  • Response IDs are not bound to a user. Another caller with access to the same project who obtains a previous_response_id can continue the response and pull its history into context. Keep IDs confidential and map them server-side to users or tenants.
  • Old versions stay callable. Invocation permission is per agent, not per version. To withdraw an unsafe configuration, delete the affected versions, and rotate any credential that was exposed.
  • Projects do not isolate every file. Batch and fine-tuning work at the parent resource scope. For independent file access, use separate Foundry resources.
  • Hub-based projects: the built-in Reader role can expose stored agent content through the legacy endpoint; grant it carefully.

Abuse monitoring for models sold by Azure detects recurring content or behavior that suggests use in a way that might violate the Code of Conduct. It has four parts:

  1. Content classificationClassifier models detect harmful text and images in prompts and completions, assign severity levels, and feed signals into pattern detection.
  2. Abuse pattern captureAlgorithms and heuristics score indicators across your usage: how often and how severe harmful content is, and whether it looks intentional (for example recurring jailbreak attempts).
  3. Review and decisionFlagged content or suspicious patterns may be sampled for review. By default the review is automated, using AI models such as LLMs, and prompts and completions reviewed this way are not stored by the system or used to train models. If automated review is not confident enough, or is unavailable, authorized Microsoft employees may review the flagged content.
  4. Notification and actionWhen abuse is confirmed you are told by email. Except for severe or recurring abuse, you typically get a chance to explain or fix it. Failing to address it, or severe or recurring abuse, may lead to suspension or termination of access.

Who can see flagged content

  • Only data already flagged, or part of a flagged pattern, can reach a human reviewer.
  • Reviewers are authorized Microsoft employees using point-wise queries by request ID, Secure Access Workstations and just-in-time approval from team managers.
  • For models deployed in the European Economic Area, those employees are located in the EEA.

Where it is kept

  • The abuse monitoring data store is logically separated per customer resource (each request carries the Foundry resource ID).
  • A separate store exists in each geography where the model is available, and your content is stored in the geography of your resource, inside the service boundary.

Modified abuse monitoring

If you process highly sensitive data, or cannot permit Microsoft to store and human-review prompts and completions, you can apply for modified abuse monitoring through the Limited Access process (some advanced models have stricter criteria). If approved, the data storage and human review are not performed. Automated review can still happen, and severe or recurring abuse may still lead to limits on access. Detection may be less accurate, so be ready to respond to a notification.

# Verify that data storage for abuse monitoring is off (after approval)
az cognitiveservices account show -n <resource-name> -g <resource-group>
# Look for the capability:  { "name": "ContentLogging", "value": "false" }
# The attribute appears ONLY when storage is off; if it is absent, storage is on.

Abuse monitoring for third-party models is run by the provider, not by Microsoft's abuse monitoring system described in the previous tab. For Claude, the docs say:

  • Anthropic safety systems and safeguards are active in both hosting options, supported by Microsoft. Harmful content screening, safety review and Anthropic-specific processing are described in Anthropic's documentation and in the terms shown at deployment.
  • Hosted on Azure: automatic safeguards flag content that might be sent to Anthropic Trust & Safety for review, and Anthropic personnel review customer content on an exceptions-only basis to investigate potential safety violations, subject to Anthropic's terms.
  • Data retention is governed by Anthropic's Data Processing Addendum and Commercial Terms of Service, not by the Microsoft abuse monitoring data store.
  • Your use must comply with Anthropic's Acceptable Use Policy.
  • Foundry does not provide built-in content filtering for Claude at deployment time. Configure Azure AI Content Safety during inference if you need Foundry-style guardrails.

The docs do not publish a per-model abuse-monitoring description for every partner model. For any model outside Claude and Fireworks, check the model card and the provider's terms, and ask your Microsoft account team to confirm in writing.
  1. Match the deployment type to your residency needStandard for a single geography, Data Zone for US or EU, Global for broadest capacity. Remember data at rest stays in your geography either way.
  2. Prefer Microsoft Entra ID and RBAC over keysAgents, evaluations and datasets require Entra ID anyway. Use customer-managed keys where policy requires them.
  3. Decide what you storeResponses API history, files, stored completions and traces all persist data. Set retention and delete what you do not need. Trace content can be restricted.
  4. Use standard agent setup when data must stay in your resourcesBring your own Storage, AI Search and Cosmos DB, and use separate Foundry resources where resource-scoped capabilities need isolation.
  5. Protect response IDs and prune agent versionsTreat IDs as sensitive, and delete versions that held unsafe configurations.
  6. For highly sensitive workloads, consider modified abuse monitoringCheck eligibility under Limited Access, and verify ContentLogging is false after approval.
  7. Choose Claude hosting deliberatelyHosted on Azure if you need residency in a geography or Data Zone (US). Add Content Safety because there is no built-in deployment-time filtering.
  8. Review every non-Microsoft termMarketplace offers, partner models, third-party MCP servers and Bing grounding each carry their own terms. Record the decision.
  9. Layer on Microsoft Purview and DefenderAuditing, sensitivity classification and DSPM for AI give visibility into prompt and response data; Defender detects jailbreak and input attacks.
  10. Disable preview features in productionPreview features may follow different privacy practices.
